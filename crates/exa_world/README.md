# `exa_world`

Levels in the Bevy world.

**Phase:** 2. Status: props, level scene nodes and lights spawn
(`spawn_level`, `spawn_model`); walls and floors from the tile grid, terrain
and the grid's rooms; portal clipping is next (see
[`docs/PLAN.md`](../../docs/PLAN.md)).

- `spawn_model(commands, model, placement)`: a cached model with its node hierarchy, its lights, fires and voxel lights (`exa_render::gi::VoxelLight`) and its nodes' keyframe tracks (`exa_anim::tracks::NodeTracks`); a placement replaces the root transform, as level props do. Placed items dim their voxel lights and glow meshes by their condition byte. `spawn_model_nodes` also returns the node entities.
- `db_objects`: the object manager's scene decisions: the scene tree (a model, a factory weapon, the invisible container volume
  with its radius, or the 50 cm placeholder of an operative record without
  a model name), the managed node flags (`0x4000`, children `0x5000` and
  the parent's hidden bit), the door and operative kind words, the
  material rules (a weapon record hides `*`, an item-body record hides
  `*` and paints `&`), the impact-sound class, masters kept per model
  unless painted, and the release that logs a mismatch and goes on. Only
  the placeholder is spawned; the rest is not wired.
- `bodies`: which rigid bodies of a level register in the solver, as the original registers them (each body tree from its top body down through its body children), and their registration: the free and hung trees of props not placed static (`Prop::STATIC`; nodes marked static, `rfc::Node::STATIC`, never register), of placed objects, and of the level's own model, asleep; a hung tree's root keeps its stored links as world links anchored in its parent node (`exa_physics::body::WorldLink`; a root's slider keeps its travel as slack, a child's slider becomes plain slack of its stored value), an origin pivot unless the first is a slider, and no level detection; a mechanism's hung trees register the same way with their rules (`placement_of`, `LevelLoad::mechanism_bodies`, `MechanismBodies`): a door's hinged leaves (`Placement::Door`, an `exa_physics::body::PendingDoor`; exanima01 has 109; off with the game's `--static-doors`), a lever's arm (`Placement::Lever`, `PendingLever`; 40) and a push surface (`Placement::Plate`, `PendingPlate`; 12), both off with `--static-mechanisms`, and, only with `--sliding-doors`, a door's sliding leaves (`PendingSlide`; 37); any other mechanism tree stays static (`LevelInfo::bodies` counts each). A body's stored scrape and roll sound slots travel with its registration (`PendingScrapes`) and become `Ragdoll` sources on their parts; the root also carries `BodySounds`, each part's material collision sound set (`NodeRigidBody::collision_sound`, from the node's first mesh material) and each slot's sound name, which `exa_audio` plays. Each registered part takes its node mesh's triangles in the node's frame (metres, `ModelNode::triangles` from `exa_assets::mesh_triangles`) as the collision mesh the pair stage tests the other side's spheres against (the prop is the mesh side whenever it is the pair's less mobile part); a node without a usable mesh leaves the sphere fallback.
- `mechanisms`: the door and operative records a placed object carries (`MechanismRecords`, under its tagged placed id), converted for `exa_physics`'s door, lever, plate and signal rules. Hinged door leaves, lever arms and push surfaces are bodies ticking their rules (`exa_physics::body::{DoorBody, LeverBody, PlateBody}`; sliding leaves only when asked, `SlideBody`). `MechanismPlugin`'s `route_signals` runs each `exa_physics::mechanism::MechanismSignal` a lever or plate sends on its target's records: a door order (open, close and lock, toggle) on the door bodies under the target's node, each woken, or a class 5 push on its push surface; relays and deferred jobs (fires, lights) are logged, not run. `OpenDoorsUnder` opens the door bodies under a placed object by its tagged id, as the player's deferred consumable use calls it (no signal inversion); the first door found under the node sounds its `LkO` lock sound once (`exa_physics::mechanism::LockSounded`, at the leaf's origin, a guess for the controller's point).
- `markers`: placement markers. A model node whose name starts with `[` (the original's sector attach for a node with no object record; an object's volume flags reading `0x22` in their second byte would also count, but none ships) gets `PlacementMarkerNode` (the eight name characters after the `[` and the link byte after them), which the item cursor snaps a lifted object onto; placed objects carry `PlacedTemplate`, their record header's parent word, beside `PlacedItem`. The shipped markers are the torch holders' `[0000001F]` and the teleporter's and resurrection device's `[000001F4L]`/`[000001AFL]`.
- `ObjectSections`: a placed object's placement word and its record's usable and container sections (`exa_formats::objdb::Item::container`), put on the object's root when it has either; the player's double click reads them, and the container popup saves its panel back into the container's children. exanima01 places 71 containers holding 307 items (128 of them the level's own records). `LevelLocalItems` keeps the loaded level's own database as a resource for the item catalogue, and `LevelLocalRecords` its records as stored (`exa_formats::rfc::Scene::local_records`) with its string table, from which the catalogue builds the level's own description cards.
- `player_markers`: a level's transition markers, read from the volume sections of its placed objects (`exa_formats::objdb::Volume`): the entrance, the id of the level they lead back from and their world position. 54 ship across the levels; `exanima01` has the box that leads back from `exanima02`.
- `spawn_level(commands, loader, name)`: the level's own meshes and omni lights plus every prop from `Objlib.rpk`, with the placement's node overrides (a named node's transform, mesh positions, or a whole named transform tree, `exa_formats::level::PropAttachment`) applied before its bodies register; placed objects take the same overrides. A position list is where the level saved the mesh (a banner's settled cloth) in the level's frame, so it is taken into the node's own frame at the placement; its word is the cloth's starting countdown (`cloth::ClothStartSleep`; 0 in every shipped record, so placed banners start asleep, as in the original). A transform record is the node's frame in the level too, as the original's placement loader stores it (over the node's world matrix, the local rotation derived from the parent's): it becomes the node's local transform seen from its parent at the placement, so the overridden sacks, buckets, carts, wall rings, chains and door leaves stand where the level saved them (every exanima01 record matches the placement composed with the model's own transform to within 1 cm). Each spawned model's meshes that carry a rigid body are marked `nav::MovingMesh` (`bodies::mark_moving_meshes`; `stamped` where the placement makes the node level geometry: every node of a prop placed static, the root of a placed object with the static bits).
- `LevelLoad`: the same level built a step at a time within a time budget per step (grid, terrain, level model, props, placed objects, lights), for a loading screen. The placed objects go through the content stream's level pass (`exa_assets::content_stream`): one entry a pass, the player named by the session's arrival word and previous level (`LevelLoad::arrival`, `previous_level`; by default nothing set them), and the wait's "N missing database objects." report; `LevelInfo::missing_database_objects` and `player_focus` carry the count and the player's position.
- `level_lifecycle`: the loaded level's lifetime. Every root a level owns (the level root with its props, grid and terrain, each placed character, each generated scene body, the room shells and the sky) carries `LevelEntity`. A `LevelChangeRequest` despawns those subtrees, empties the physics world (`exa_physics::Physics::clear`), removes the old capsules so the fixed step makes new ones, and drops the level's GI blocks and textures, navigation grid, room and wall resources, terrain ground, fitted sun map, sky and fog. The host then loads the destination through `LevelLoad`; `LevelChangeReady` carries its name and spawn. The player, camera and interface are not level entities, so the session state survives.
- `PlacedCharacter`: a level's placed character resolved for spawning (position, facing, dead or alive, race, worn and held items) with its whole record, from which play mode derives its stats, and the encumbrance its worn items and held shield give it and its worn items' special kinds (`WornSpecials`) (the level's or the game's items). A dead placement also carries its saved pose (a bone tree or the legacy flat frames) and a flag for the command line's synthetic corpse, the only dead body built standing and left to fall. A world-database character is kept only in its own locale (`locales.rdb`), as the original creates them.
- `teleporters`: the teleporter and reconstruction device rules. A device's first `[` node is its crystal slot; a scene object within 1 cm of it is what it holds (NaN is never near), and a use needs the player within 100 cm of the point 16 cm above the device (NaN passes, as the original's unordered compare does) and no possession of another body. The teleporter takes a Power Crystal A (431, below 0x55 spent) or its large-crystal adapter (1423), compared by the whole record id, and lays the destination screen's rows out as the original walks them (a zero entry, or one past the free rows, goes to row 0; the shipped 7, 10, 12, 13 show as 13, 7, 10, 12; flag bit 2 shows one row) with the `prtlsmbla.rfi` atlas. The reconstruction device takes a Power Crystal L (500) whatever its charge, starts the scan 1600 ms later with the 3.0 flash, glows over a 1500 ms rise, 3800 ms hold and 1500 ms fall (`pulse_step`, in double precision) and raises the reconstruction latch with the player record's identity. `session_end` turns a story death (red below 0.01 in double) of that identity into a reconstruction, stripping only the first time: an unshared record is copied first and the dressed copy (one use, mark 0x40, identity 0, roles 1 and 0xC2, no health) is the corpse, while the player keeps the stripped record (hands and worn list cleared, one empty action, health and stamina 0x7FFF of 0xFFFF). An object let go within 5 cm² of the slot point powers the device down. The destination ids are the locale list's (7, 10, 12 and 13 are Exanima05, 07, 08 and 67); `rooms::enter_level` turns a chosen id into the level it loads. Placed objects carry `PlacedItem` (record id, the placed object's tagged id and the placement condition) and the two devices `TeleportDevice`; the database-id lookup (`object_entity`) and the promotion rewrite (`remap_placed_ids`) walk the placed roots; `exa_game::teleporters` drives them.
- `rooms`: the grid's rooms and door links (the original's flood fill), the cell a point stands in (`cell_of`, and `cell_of_node` clamped to the grid as nodes are sorted), `open_side` (the flood's "open and not a wall" side test), each link's portal (plane B's side nibble and plane C's special type, half widths and tops of 82/245, 115/290 and 200/340 cm, from −50 cm up), the door a placed node stands on, the original's light reach through open doors (2 m from the ¾ door point), its control-side door query (no room checks, so two points in no room reach; otherwise the first link of the first point's room into the second point's cell decides, with no distance), its light spill (a light within 2 m of its cell's centre reaches the rooms behind that cell's open doors), `RoomLights` (the room manager's per-room light aliases; a room that owns a light passes its whole list, deduplicated, to the rooms its portals lead into, in room order; modelled, not read by the renderer), and `RoomTracker`, the original's active, adjacent and hidden rooms around an anchor with their 1 s fades, its reset and its active-room flag (faded in, or wanted again mid fade-out).
- `scene_nodes`: the original's scene-node unit as a pure model, not yet on the draw path (`room_view` still draws from the room states). The scene is its own first sector; nodes outside every scene hang from `_World` (owner `None`). Registration by kind (meshes, lights unless world lights, auxiliary nodes per sector; geometry per scene; the owner always; `IN_SCENE` only for the listed kinds; a world light clears its fixture parts' shadow flags instead of joining the light list), batch insertion (the material queues flush first, leading sectors appended, the first other entry registers the whole batch in its owner), locked sectors, change bits and the per-sector membership rebuild (aliases the sector no longer owns are dropped, a processed one's slot taken by the last kept alias after it, so the order is not kept; meshes that left leave the mesh and per-material lists and give their bucket faces to the kept aliases' shadow maps; the kept meshes' per-alias bucket chains follow the moved aliases and link the waiting ones; aliases become processed), the light activation pass (a light whose owner is no longer the sector leaves the list, and one out of the scene also drops its aliases; a light whose state word is below 2 snapshots its position unless its light word skips it, with a zero height in the flat snapshot mode, gets its sector alias (a new one starts moved) and, in grid mode, stores its cell and is reported for the cell flood or directional fill, otherwise for an alias relink; flickering lights get a flicker step; auxiliary nodes snapshot their position the same way), subtree moves (local position plus the old origin less the new), child validation (the last child fills an empty slot), the recursive far sort-key reset (−100000), frames in sector space, a sector's two mesh boxes (seeded at the first split mesh, the second carrying on from the first, extents capped at each radius, the union radius `(x² + y²) + z²` about the world centre) and the scene's bounds written into its own sector (each room's boxes turned into world-axis extents by the room's rotation, as the active-sector test meets its box along it), the 6 m cell and spill mask (one byte, `1/600` multiplier, −1 cm/601 cm; the cell handler runs when the cell changes), the per-alias bucket list (one 24-byte entry per sector alias, rebuilt from an index or from scratch) and its face masks, and the per-frame visibility: the scene sector's full mask, active sectors against the main views, adjacent sectors through their doorways to active sectors (clipped by the first main view, the scissor from the faces' first vertices rounded ties-to-even), the node passes over the processed meshes (the hidden pass for no view bit and no such doorway keeps only the top mask bit; in both passes a mesh whose state word says it just moved stores its cell, runs every alias's shadow test and is relinked when it needs buckets, and other bucket meshes test the moved aliases), the 20 cm doorway test against the sector beyond, the inherited frame bits and the cut-away fade (0.003 per whole millisecond; 0.001 and 0.999 in double precision; fading back in sets the dithered bit whether or not the mesh had the main bit). `frame` runs the original's order (the removal ring drained, classification, activation in every sector, membership, new buckets, node passes, removals, material removes) and returns the render-side work as `FrameEvents`. Its cut-away detector (`detect_cut_away`) probes the meshes of the sectors the first view draws, in each sector's space: the camera's segment (from beyond the eye to a metre short of the player) against each candidate's bounding sphere, or a 10 m segment straight down onto the player for an overhead one, by the kind bits of the scene word; marks carry to the parent and the mesh children, and a miss clears a marked mesh. It shares [`scene_nodes::cell_spill`] with `light_flood`.
- `light_flood`: a directional light fills `width²` cells with an empty window (cells that list it already keep theirs); the mesh cell and spill test is the scene-node unit's `cell_spill`.
- `room_view`: the rooms in the Bevy world: `LevelRooms`, `RoomAnchor` (the player, or 20 m ahead of the fly camera), `InRoom` on meshes, rooms of props and characters, and the rooms' black shells (`RoomShell`: occluder, lid, fade overlay) shown by state; `RoomView { all }` shows every room.
- `grid`: walls and floors from the wall and floor sets, and each room's occluder and lid merged from the pieces' `[…` and `+R…` strips and the ceiling slots (never spawned as pieces); ceiling caps; the per-cell floor mapping is the original's centimetre multiply (`× 1/600`, then ±½), and a side's quarter turn comes from the engine's fast sine/cosine pair (`side_rotation`), so a turn is exact. Special tile masks turn with the cell, a cell past the last piece of its type reuses it (and one with none of its type takes the set's first), types 6–9 keep their centre floor, a floor set named `Terrain` is not loaded, pieces and characters look their room up by the cell they stand in, the wall and floor set tables are looked up by index, and nodes sort into the room sectors with the original's floored, clamped cell lookup and the default sector (`sector_lists`, the node distribution and sector export). `Strip::ray_hits` is the engine's one-sided mesh ray test (front faces only, determinant at least the f32 epsilon, 0 < t < limit), and `grid::ray_blocks` / `segment_blocks` walk the active rooms' occluder or lid shells in order, answering no at the first active room that has none, as the room manager's queries do (their picking and segment callers are not ported).
- `grid_sets`: the builder's set catalog: the wall and floor set names registered once each (whole-name comparison, the original's 16-byte name), the loaded flag, and the `Terrain` floor set marked `0x7E01` instead of loaded by the load pass, so it never brings floor pieces (the original's floor registration loads a new set at once, before that pass; see `docs/formats/RFC.md`). `GridBuild` registers every name it loads and indexes a set shared by name once.
- `scene_env`: the original's scene environment as a pure model: the sun and moon on their 45° path and the daylight factor (the engine's fast sines, the library's double cosines and its approximate reciprocal square root), the engine's built-in six-record day track and its start-up pass (the `o` fill rule; the optional step that linearises the colours and scales the sky colours by 0.75 is modelled but off, as the shipped game never sets its flag), the records' pick-and-blend, the engine's own sRGB curve (the fast logarithm and exponential), cloud dimming (the luminance summed x, y, then z; `f⁴` squared twice), the fog's span and per-frame near/far, the clear colour (RGBA) and sky gradient, the ambient scale, the automatic sunlight rule, the sun mode and sky switch (the light's colour and intensity back to the level's and 3), the sun placement's two bodies for the sky, the scene's `k`-colour scale, the sky cube's tint, the flash tint, the frame's sun-height value (−100000 or −25), its rain-driven wind, tint and sun intensity, the `_Sunlight` node's constants, rain, a rain effect and sound, thunder and lightning with the original's generators and its branch order, the paused and view-led tick, the strike placed 200 cm in front of the view, and the save unit's three presets: the indoor/underground scene, the seeded outdoor day and weather, and the fog variants. `environment` turns it into the level's `Lighting` (ambient, sun, linear fog, clear colour and sky) and Bevy resources; a level load calls `level_lighting_scene`, which applies the preset the level's name selects (indoor for `exanima*` and the first seven arenas, the seeded outdoor preset for `arenao*`, the record for everything else) with a seed from the process clock, keeps the environment as `SceneEnvRuntime` with the engine's and the library's streams, and a `PostUpdate` system ticks the weather each frame and rewrites the fog, clear colour, ambient and sun light only when the frame changes (the time of day still has no speed in play, so it does not advance by itself).

- `voxeliser`: the GI sample-point generator's parameters: a 40 cm lattice spacing, the 4.0 and 0.4 tuning values and one work pass.
- `gi_volume`: the voxel GI occupancy block, built on the async compute pool for the block the camera is in; each grid cell's floor meshes are merged and get the original's floor heightfield points (`exa_physics::voxel`) once per level, in the cell's own space, and `GiState` tells a loading screen where it stands. `GiBlockLayout` lays the level's blocks out once (the built cells' bounding box rounded to eight-cell blocks and slid inside the grid; the synthetic 8 × 8 grid and its block at cells (3, 3) for a level without one), the camera picks the block its cell falls in, and every built occupancy is kept for the level (`gi_residency::GiResidency`, the original's residency: the first nine blocks hold the light textures, built blocks are ranked by their box distance from the focus with a stable insertion sort, the next block to build is the first unbuilt or dirty one in that order and is built in the background while the camera's block stands, and a near built block without a texture takes the farthest textured block's), and `GiBlockLayout::sample_transform`/`sample_at` give the original's per-object sample offset and scale (half a voxel up; the flag drops it). Thin and centred GI meshes splat their quarter weight or half-voxel height (the centred flag is the node's flags2 `0x10`; a mesh is thin when its first surface's material has render class bit `0x4000` or an opacity layer with opacity above 0.75, `Material::is_thin_for_gi`, both kept by the model reader); the environment record's flag `0x20` lets the light steps draw the top slice (`PendingGi::top_slice`); `Occupancy::update_dynamic` takes a moving mesh's old points out and puts its new ones in; `VoxelRegion`/`sub_upload` carry the dirty bytes. Every frame it marks the cells within the reach that are built and in view, then the built cells next to a marked one, in the original's two passes, and lists each sector's lights from the 6 m cells' flood lists (`exa_render::gi::GiSectorLights`).
- `point_light(light)`: the original omni light approximated as a Bevy `PointLight`.
- `light_source`: the state a light node keeps beside its record (base and current intensity, scale, the flicker term, the record's or the constructor's amplitude, speed and emissive share, the shadow-quality offset), the lever, and the once-a-frame flicker and fade at the scene's frame time with the engine's generator, in the original's double-precision steps; a flickering light drives its point light and its parent mesh's first part's emissive (on a material copy of its own).
- `editor`: the dormant in-game object editor's look roll (`roll`): a pair of palette colours (family × 8 + shade), a tone, a style, a material set and finish, and a flag, drawn from the engine's integer and unit streams in the original's order; not wired, the editor itself is not ported.
- `node_parts`: a model node's part entities in the model's part order (the original's sub-object list).
- `particles`: the original's CPU particle emitter (`TParticleSystem`): its template record and size scaling, derived per-tick values, ring of Verlet particles, ageing (the fire's distance cap), emission with its two-thirds accumulator, light and sound walks, quad building (diamond, stretched, billboard) with the atlas variants and colour keys, and body contacts; `particle_uv_table` builds the particle unit's 128-entry UV lookup (four atlas cells of eight symmetries of four corners) and is tested against the per-particle variant arithmetic. The draws come from the engine's integer LCG and the run-time library's Mersenne twister (`Rng`, `Mt19937`), and the arithmetic follows the original's (double-precision steps, half-to-even rounding). Plain state and rules at the 83 Hz tick; no Bevy `App` needed for a test.
- `fire`: the fire presets: the six templates and three beds, the size tiers (small, medium-small, medium and large at half size), `CreateFire`'s light (including the ×1.25 radius above size 0.2 in double precision), the ring sources, burning items' sizes and the marker import.
- `flame`: the fire emitters in the Bevy world: their fixed ticks (four catch-up ticks at most), the fire's light density and softness, and each frame's particle mesh with the `_FirePrt` material, skipped while the flame is hidden; a finished one-shot fire is removed. The two particle textures load through the texture manager's streaming path, so an emitter draws the loader's placeholder until its upload arrives and the flame material is rebound when the image handle's contents change. `CarriedFire` on a model root (the carried torch) makes its fires ride the item as the original's body-attached emitters do.
- `arc`: electric arcs in the Bevy world, over `exa_render::electricity`'s rules: the jittered tendrils' camera-facing ribbon with the `_ElecMat01` material and the preset's light (a voxel light, or a device's point light). Nothing spawns an arc yet.
- `sun_shadow`: the sun's one fitted orthographic shadow map in a level with a terrain: each frame it clips every active camera's frustum (out to the original camera's 125 m far plane) at the level's box and the record's floor and ceiling on the sides the camera looks towards, fits it with `exa_render::sun_fit` (200 cm radius steps with hysteresis, texel-snapped centre, ±8000 cm depth) and writes the matrices into the sun's single Bevy cascade before Bevy derives the shadow frusta; a frame that fits nothing keeps the last map. Other levels, and `--bevy-sun-cascades`, keep Bevy's two cascades ([`docs/RENDERING.md`](../../docs/RENDERING.md), Sun shadow fit).
- `cloth`: the level's cloth-marked meshes (long hair, four banners, the wraith's robe) become a simulated cloth (`ClothInstance`) with one particle per position element of the mesh (`ClothSource` carries the model's `ClothMesh`;
