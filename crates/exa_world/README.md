# `exa_world`

Levels in the Bevy world.

**Phase:** 2. Status: props, level scene nodes and lights spawn
(`spawn_level`, `spawn_model`); walls and floors from the tile grid, terrain
and the grid's rooms; portal clipping is next (see
[`docs/PLAN.md`](../../docs/PLAN.md)).

- `spawn_model(commands, model, placement)`: a cached model with its node hierarchy, its lights, fires and voxel lights (`exa_render::gi::VoxelLight`) and its nodes' keyframe tracks (`exa_anim::tracks::NodeTracks`); a placement replaces the root transform, as level props do. Placed items dim their voxel lights and glow meshes by their condition byte; a condition-scaled one keeps `ConditionLit` (its records' light intensities and its own glow materials) and `rescale_condition_lights` scales them again whenever its condition changes (a crystal's charge). `spawn_model_nodes` also returns the node entities.
- `db_objects`: the object manager's scene decisions: the scene tree (a model, a factory weapon, the invisible container volume
  with its radius, or the 50 cm placeholder of an operative record without
  a model name), the managed node flags (`0x4000`, children `0x5000` and
  the parent's hidden bit), the door and operative kind words and the kind the built node
  carries (`node_kind`: the tree's kind on a model, the volume kind on a
  container volume, the operative kind on the placeholder; the content
  stream's level pass dispatches on it to find the player), the
  material rules (a weapon record hides `*`, an item-body record hides
  `*` and paints `&`), the impact-sound class, masters kept per model
  unless painted, the release that logs a mismatch and goes on, and the
  paint seed of the object database's oldest body layout (two Mersenne
  twister words, the second's top bit cleared, low word first). The spawn
  builds a model-less record's invisible container volume (its extents and
  radius) or, for an operative record without a volume, the 50 cm
  placeholder, so the level pass has a node for those records; `node_kind`
  is read by the level pass, and the rest is not wired.
- `bodies`: which rigid bodies of a level register in the solver, as the original registers them (each body tree from its top body down through its body children), and their registration: the free and hung trees of props not placed static (`Prop::STATIC`; nodes marked static, `rfc::Node::STATIC`, never register), of placed objects, and of the level's own model, asleep; a hung tree's root keeps its stored links as world links anchored in its parent node (`exa_physics::body::WorldLink`; a root's slider keeps its travel as slack, a child's slider becomes plain slack of its stored value), an origin pivot unless the first is a slider, and no level detection; a mechanism's hung trees register the same way with their rules (`placement_of`, `LevelLoad::mechanism_bodies`, `MechanismBodies`): a door's hinged leaves (`Placement::Door`, an `exa_physics::body::PendingDoor`; exanima01 has 109; off with the game's `--static-doors`), a lever's arm (`Placement::Lever`, `PendingLever`; 40) and a push surface (`Placement::Plate`, `PendingPlate`; 12), both off with `--static-mechanisms`, and a door's sliding leaves (`PendingSlide`; 37, exanima01's portcullises, off with `--static-sliding-doors`); a lever's registration also finds the first light node under its placed model (`PendingLever::light`) so a kind 6 lever fades it; any other mechanism tree stays static (`LevelInfo::bodies` counts each). A body's stored scrape and roll sound slots travel with its registration (`PendingScrapes`) and become `Ragdoll` sources on their parts; the root also carries `BodySounds`, each part's material collision sound set (`NodeRigidBody::collision_sound`, from the node's first mesh material) and each slot's sound name, which `exa_audio` plays; a door leaf's also names its record's slam set as the fallback (`BodySounds::fallback`). Each registered part takes its node mesh's triangles in the node's frame (metres, `ModelNode::triangles` from `exa_assets::mesh_triangles`) as the collision mesh the pair stage tests the other side's spheres against (the prop is the mesh side whenever it is the pair's less mobile part); a node without a usable mesh leaves the sphere fallback.
- `mechanisms`: the door and operative records a placed object carries (`MechanismRecords`, under its tagged placed id), converted for `exa_physics`'s door, lever, plate and signal rules. Hinged door leaves, lever arms and push surfaces are bodies ticking their rules (`exa_physics::body::{DoorBody, LeverBody, PlateBody, SlideBody}`). `MechanismPlugin`'s `route_signals` runs each `exa_physics::mechanism::MechanismSignal` a lever or plate sends on its target's records: a door order (open, close and lock, toggle) on the door bodies under the target's node, each woken, or a class 5 push on its push surface; a class 6 fire job spawns a fire at the node at the signal's size (hundredths of the record's value, 2 for a zero value), while light jobs (they power the electric object under the node) and relays are logged, not run (no system holds electric objects or the level's listener lists). `OpenDoorsUnder` opens the door bodies under a placed object by its tagged id, as the player's deferred consumable use calls it (no signal inversion); the order sounds as a signal's does. A signal's door order plays the lock set the original plays (an open: the first door's once; a close and lock: each unlatched sliding leaf's; `exa_physics::mechanism::LockSounded`, at the leaf's origin, a guess for the controller's point), and a shut leaf sets bit 0 of the group's word. `LatchDoorsAt` latches or unlatches the doors of the first door object within 10 cm (x and z) of a spot, waiting for its body (the arena's physician door). A placement's static bit marks the placed object's whole tree level geometry (`bodies::placement_geometry`). `EXA_MECHANISM_LIST=1` (debug) lists every sliding leaf (place, state and whether it stands nearer its placed pose, in its arch, or its rail's far end, clear of it: the door's `closed` is the original's flag for the far end) and lever (place, state, targets) once the level stands, then the awake leaves' fractions every 30 frames. A fire record's load set-up and the record flag `0x40`'s switch-off put the fixture's glow out (`put_out_glow`, `switch_off`: the first part's, or every part's, emissive and each mesh child's first part; a voxel-light child's intensity without the colour update).
- `markers`: placement markers. A model node whose name starts with `[` (the original's sector attach for a node with no object record; an object's volume flags reading `0x22` in their second byte would also count, but none ships) gets `PlacementMarkerNode` (the eight name characters after the `[` and the link byte after them), which the item cursor snaps a lifted object onto; placed objects carry `PlacedTemplate`, their record header's parent word, beside `PlacedItem`. The shipped markers are the torch holders' `[0000001F]` and the teleporter's and resurrection device's `[000001F4L]`/`[000001AFL]`.
- `ObjectSections`: a placed object's placement word and its record's usable section with a scripted device's use script (`exa_formats::objdb::Item::use_script`) and its container section (`exa_formats::objdb::Item::container`), put on the object's root when it has either; the player's double click reads them, and the container popup saves its panel back into the container's children. exanima01 places 71 containers holding 307 items (128 of them the level's own records). `LevelLocalItems` keeps the loaded level's own database decoded as a resource for the container fill. The level load also makes the live object database's local tier (`exa_assets::live_objects::LiveObjects`) the level's own records as stored (`exa_formats::rfc::Scene::local_database`) with its string table, as the original's level reader replaces its local tier; the item catalogue, the panels' records and the kits read them there.
- `player_markers`: a level's transition markers, read from the volume sections of its placed objects (`exa_formats::objdb::Volume`): the entrance, the id of the level they lead back from, their world position and rotation rows (a level change's arrival frame). 54 ship across the levels; `exanima01` has the box that leads back from `exanima02`.
- `alarm_volumes` and `script_volumes`: a level's trigger volumes in placement order, their box in centimetres and node radius; an alarm zone's pass, a script zone's id and routine (`LevelInfo::script_volumes`; eleven enter zones ship, `exanima01`'s `Checkpoint` among them).
- `spawn_level(commands, loader, name)`: the level's own meshes and omni lights plus every prop from `Objlib.rpk`, with the placement's node overrides (a named node's transform, mesh positions, or a whole named transform tree, `exa_formats::level::PropAttachment`) applied before its bodies register; placed objects take the same overrides. A position list is where the level saved the mesh (a banner's settled cloth) in the level's frame, so it is taken into the node's own frame at the placement; its word is the cloth's starting countdown (`cloth::ClothStartSleep`; 0 in every shipped record, so placed banners start asleep, as in the original). A transform record is the node's frame in the level too, as the original's placement loader stores it (over the node's world matrix, the local rotation derived from the parent's): it becomes the node's local transform seen from its parent at the placement, so the overridden sacks, buckets, carts, wall rings, chains and door leaves stand where the level saved them (every exanima01 record matches the placement composed with the model's own transform to within 1 cm). A prop's placement flags replace its object node's static bit and visibility rules (`Prop::NODE_FLAGS`); a model's lights and fires are spawned hidden by the rule on their root (`0x100000` hides the lights, `0x200000` the lights and fires once the level's daylight scale is above 0.12), so arenao1's two always-off lanterns stay dark and its third lantern and arenao3's braziers go out by day. Each spawned model's meshes that carry a rigid body are marked `nav::MovingMesh` (`bodies::mark_moving_meshes`; `stamped` where the placement makes the node level geometry: every node of a prop placed static, the root of a placed object with the static bits).
- `LevelLoad`: the same level built a step at a time within a time budget per step (grid, terrain, level model, props, placed objects, lights), for a loading screen. `LevelLoad::begin_from` runs the importer's archive pass (`set_archive_pass`, installed by `exa_game::archive_pass`) over a level read from the archive before anything reads its records, and the live database then takes that already migrated tier (`LiveObjects::load_level_migrated`); it reads a save's own section instead of the archive's file (the host passes `SavedLevelSection` when its locale is the level's); a finished load inserts `LevelSource` (the section's bytes, whether a save gave them and the indices of the placed objects it spawned), and each spawned placed object carries `PlacedIndex`, its place in the section's list (each prop root `PropIndex`), so a save can write the level back with the live state. The placed objects go through the content stream's level pass (`exa_assets::content_stream`): one entry a pass, the player named by the session's arrival word and previous level (`LevelLoad::arrival`, `previous_level`; by default nothing set them, a respawn's load sets the session-start word), and the wait's "N missing database objects." report; `LevelInfo::missing_database_objects`, `player_focus` and `player_frame` carry the count, the player's position and that object's frame (the respawn portal).
- `level_lifecycle`: the loaded level's lifetime. Every root a level owns (the level root with its props, grid and terrain, each placed character, each generated scene body, the room shells and the sky) carries `LevelEntity`. A `LevelChangeRequest` despawns those subtrees, empties the physics world (`exa_physics::Physics::clear`) and drops the level's GI blocks and textures and empties the GI manager's shape list, navigation grid, room and wall resources, terrain ground, fitted sun map, sky and fog, and puts the simulation's scene grid back to its stand-in (`exa_physics::SceneGrid`; the load's grid stage gives it the grid chunk's width and height, or the all-pairs step for a level without one). The host then loads the destination through `LevelLoad`; `LevelChangeReady` carries its name, spawn and whether it is a door's change (no `WorldReset` with it). The player, camera and interface are not level entities, so the session state survives. `BeforeDoorTeardown` lists one-shot systems the teardown runs first, queued ahead of its despawns, for a change to a level that no `WorldReset` came with (the save unit's follower migration). `WorldReset` marks a change behind the original's world reset (a new session, a save load, an arena round, a scripted jump, a quit; not a door's change), for the state that reset clears (the weapons' blood list).
- `PlacedCharacter`: a level's placed character resolved for spawning (position, facing, dead or alive, race, worn and held items) with its whole record, from which play mode derives its stats, and the encumbrance its worn items and held shield give it and its worn items' special kinds (`WornSpecials`) (the level's or the game's items). A dead placement also carries its saved pose (a bone tree or the legacy flat frames), a saved placement its motion state and AI home, and a flag for the command line's synthetic corpse, the only dead body built standing and left to fall. A placement whose reference has the world bit is looked up in the live world character database the host hands the load (`LevelLoad::world_characters`), never in the level's own records, and created only in its own locale (`locales.rdb`), as the original's character reader does; no shipped level has such a placement.
- `saved_parts`: each placed object's and prop's spawned root carries its model's tree (`PartTree`: each node's entity, parent, name, the attachment its model node carries, body, cloth or emitter, and its local transform as the load placed it), which a save's part records are written from (`exa_game::level_save::part_records`); a cloth keeps the places it was built at (`cloth::ClothInstance::start`) so the save can tell a moved cloth.
- `teleporters`: the teleporter and reconstruction device rules. A device's first `[` node is its crystal slot; a scene object within 1 cm of it is what it holds (NaN is never near), and a use needs the player within 100 cm of the point 16 cm above the device (NaN passes, as the original's unordered compare does) and no possession of another body. The teleporter takes a Power Crystal A (431, below 0x55 spent) or its large-crystal adapter (1423), compared by the whole record id, and lays the destination screen's rows out as the original walks them (a zero entry, or one past the free rows, goes to row 0; the shipped 7, 10, 12, 13 show as 13, 7, 10, 12; flag bit 2 shows one row) with the `prtlsmbla.rfi` atlas. The reconstruction device takes a Power Crystal L (500) whatever its charge, starts the scan 1600 ms later with the 3.0 flash, glows over a 1500 ms rise, 3800 ms hold and 1500 ms fall (`pulse_step`, in double precision) and raises the reconstruction latch with the player record's identity. `Latch` is that word (idle, charging or reconstructed) with the identity it remembers, the same word the story session's death handler reads; `session_end` turns a story death (red below 0.01 in double) of that identity into a reconstruction, stripping only the first time: an unshared record is copied first and the dressed copy (one use, mark 0x40, identity 0, roles 1 and 0xC2, no health) is the corpse, while the player keeps the stripped record (hands, worn list and carried list cleared, health and stamina 0x7FFF of 0xFFFF; `exa_game::left_behind` runs it on the live records). An object let go within 5 cm² of the slot point powers the device down. The destination ids are the locale list's (7, 10, 12 and 13 are Exanima05, 07, 08 and 67); `rooms::enter_level` turns a chosen id into the level it loads. Placed objects carry `PlacedItem` (record id, the placed object's tagged id and the placement condition) and the two devices `TeleportDevice`; the database-id lookup (`object_entity`) and the promotion rewrite (`remap_placed_ids`) walk the placed roots; `exa_game::teleporters` drives them.
- `rooms`: the grid's rooms and door links (the original's flood fill), the cell a point stands in (`cell_of`, `cell_of_node` clamped to the grid as nodes are sorted, and `cell_of_moving_node` clamped one cell in from the edge, the cell bytes the scene's per-frame cell update writes onto a moving root or light, whose room hook `scene_live` runs only when those bytes change), `open_side` (the flood's "open and not a wall" side test), each link's portal (plane B's side nibble and plane C's special type, half widths and tops of 82/245, 115/290 and 200/340 cm, from −50 cm up), the door a placed node stands on and `DoorBodies`, which binds a placed door's body to both sides of its link (the original's cell and third-row facing step), `link_open` (a link is open without a body or while its body's pair flag is clear, so a locked door counts as open), the original's light reach through open doors (2 m from the ¾ door point), its control-side door query (no room checks, so two points in no room reach; otherwise the first link of the first point's room into the second point's cell decides, with no distance), its light spill (a light within 2 m of its cell's centre reaches the rooms behind that cell's open doors), `RoomLights` (the room manager's per-room light aliases: live, a light is placed in its sector (the room of its cell, else the scene's own) and added to the room of every cell its flood records it in, the cell hook, and `shadow_links` links a mesh only to the lights its sector lists; the load-time `propagate`, a room that owns a light passing its whole list to the rooms its portals lead into, has no alias maker behind it in the binary and only the terrain's hidden-block ranking runs it), and `RoomTracker`, the original's active, adjacent and hidden rooms around an anchor with their 1 s fades, its reset and its active-room flag (faded in, or wanted again mid fade-out); the anchor's cell is its linear index against the cell count and a door point's reach a squared compare, and the fade overlay's alpha is taken in double and ends an entering fade when its single, widened, is under 0.01, as the original's walk and fade pass compute them; `update_forced` is the walk's forced-room branch (the stored room alone: its door test measures from minus the largest float, which never reaches a door). The rooms keep the grid's size and each cell's plane A word (`width`, `height`, `cell_word`), which the story's exploration reads (`exa_game::experience_live::RoomCells`).
- `start::cast_onto_floor`: the original's start of play without a player: the level's own start lifted 10 m and line-cast 30 m down onto the level's front faces (`exa_physics::Physics::line_cast`); the player is added at the hit (a miss keeps the start, where the original keeps the lifted point). Floor nodes' parts built from the mesh group the index list starts with carry `exa_physics::FirstFloorPart` (`ModelNode::first_part`), whose triangle 0 the Ground Query ignores; a grid cell's merged floor parts never do. `start::arena_snap` is the arena spawners' snap: the same cast, the body 5 cm over the hit (`exa_game::scene_bodies::SceneBodyPlan::snap`; the player's spot in elimination and reserve matches).
- `cut_away`: the camera's cut-away fade on spawned models: the `CutAway` record the model spawner attaches to every candidate mesh node and to the meshes its marks carry to (word, triangles, sphere, parent and children carries, parts, mark, fade and target; grid set pieces get theirs without carries), and the two mesh callbacks the detector asks after the bounding sphere: `sphere_cast` (the segment kind's 50 cm swept sphere) and `ray_cast` (the overhead kind's one-sided ray). The spawner also attaches a `WallObstacle` to every mesh the camera's wall probe meets (no simulated body, triangles kept): `WallObstacle::meets` asks the probe's segment against the bounding sphere, then the swept sphere from the far end for kind `0x5…` and the ray for the rest. Grid set pieces get theirs too (`grid::spawn_node`): the tileset loader's mark makes them obstacles. `exa_game::cut_away_live` drives the cut-away, the chase camera the probe; rules: `docs/RENDERING.md` (Cut-away fade).
- `scene_nodes`: the original's scene-node unit; `scene_live` keeps one for the loaded grid level and runs its frame (below). `quiet_passes` is an Exarust shortcut for that large live scene: a sector whose last pass was the hidden pass and whose lists did not change visits only the meshes refreshed since, and a node whose children did not change skips their validation (the same result, but for the order of the moved meshes in the frame's events). The scene is its own first sector; nodes outside every scene hang from `_World` (owner `None`). Registration by kind (meshes, lights unless world lights, auxiliary nodes per sector; geometry per scene; the owner always; `IN_SCENE` only for the listed kinds; a world light clears its fixture parts' shadow flags instead of joining the light list), batch insertion (the material queues flush first, leading sectors appended, the first other entry registers the whole batch in its owner), locked sectors, change bits and the per-sector membership rebuild (aliases the sector no longer owns are dropped, a processed one's slot taken by the last kept alias after it, so the order is not kept; meshes that left leave the mesh and per-material lists and give their bucket faces to the kept aliases' shadow maps; the kept meshes' per-alias bucket chains follow the moved aliases and link the waiting ones; aliases become processed), the light activation pass (a light whose owner is no longer the sector leaves the list, and one out of the scene also drops its aliases; a light whose state word is below 2 snapshots its position unless its light word skips it, with a zero height in the flat snapshot mode, gets its sector alias (a new one starts moved) and, in grid mode, stores its cell and is flooded through the caller's flood (`activate_lights_with`: the rooms whose cells recorded it get the cell hook, then unstamped aliases go), otherwise its alias spreads and is reported for a relink; flickering lights get a flicker step; auxiliary nodes snapshot their position the same way), subtree moves (local position plus the old origin less the new), child validation (the last child fills an empty slot), the recursive far sort-key reset (−100000), frames in sector space (and the scene's snapshot callback, which copies a node's world frame into its previous frame; its caller, the sector's per-frame node pass, is not modelled), a sector's two mesh boxes (seeded at the first split mesh, the second carrying on from the first, extents capped at each radius, the union radius `(x² + y²) + z²` about the world centre) and the scene's bounds written into its own sector (each room's boxes turned into world-axis extents by the room's rotation, as the active-sector test meets its box along it), the 6 m cell and spill mask (one byte, `1/600` multiplier, −1 cm/601 cm; the cell handler runs when the cell changes), the per-alias bucket list (one 24-byte entry per sector alias, rebuilt from an index or from scratch) with its weights and face masks, filled by the alias link methods (`alias_reach`: the alias constructor's choice on the light's ranged bit, the gated reach with the caller's wall gate in grid mode or the plain luma test, against the mesh's colour bound, `SceneNode::colour`; a gated miss leaves the alias's shadow word alone, a gated hit past the intensity gate merges the new and old faces into it for a shadow-casting mesh, a lit plain test sets it to 1; the plain test's unlit answer is the original's stray stack byte, modelled as lit), ordered heaviest first after the link tests by the original's insertion sort (`order_buckets`: each entry is taken off the chain and walked back over the lighter ones until one is at least as heavy, equal weights staying and a NaN on either side of a compare stopping the walk), a light's alias list (the sectors holding its aliases, in the order they were made; `remove_alias` swaps the last into the freed slot, marks the sector and clears the alias's owner), the activation's alias sync (`sync_alias_links`: after the flood, every alias of the light takes the light's values and link values at its frame, the light's node under its sector, and is marked moved, so the node passes run its link tests; `alias_sync` turns it off to compare), the room cell hook (`flood_hook`: the light's alias in the room's sector stamped with the frame counter, or a new one made) and the flood's last step (`drop_unflooded`: aliases no cell stamped this frame are removed, walking the list as it stood), the alias a light finds or makes in another sector (a ranged light, `LIGHT_RANGED`, the level record's node flag `0x40000`, only where its sphere reaches the sector's box, the sphere test first and then the squared point-to-box distance against the squared radius; a current stamp prunes the walk, an older one refreshes the alias in place and marks it moved, and a gated light's new alias carries a shadow map needing all six faces) and its spread along the sectors' linked lists (the sector marked busy so a loop ends), which the activation pass runs outside grid mode (no sector is linked yet, so the running game spreads nothing), and the per-frame visibility: the scene sector's full mask, active sectors against the main views, adjacent sectors through their doorways to active sectors (clipped by the first main view, the scissor from the faces' first vertices rounded ties-to-even), the node passes over the processed meshes (the hidden pass for no view bit and no such doorway keeps only the top mask bit; in both passes a mesh whose state word says it just moved stores its cell, runs every alias's link test (the caller's `AliasLink`: weight, faces, lit) and orders its buckets when it needs them, and other bucket meshes test the moved aliases and are ordered again when one lit them), the 20 cm doorway test against the sector beyond, the inherited frame bits and the cut-away fade (0.003 per whole millisecond; 0.001 and 0.999 in double precision; fading back in sets the dithered bit whether or not the mesh had the main bit). `frame` runs the original's order (the removal ring drained, classification, activation in every sector, membership, new buckets, node passes, removals, material removes) and returns the render-side work as `FrameEvents`. `destroy` empties the lists the original's destructor frees. Its cut-away detector (`detect_cut_away`) probes the meshes of the sectors the first view draws, in each sector's space: the camera's segment (from beyond the eye to a metre short of the player) against each candidate's bounding sphere, or a 10 m segment straight down onto the player for an overhead one, by the kind bits of the scene word; a mark carries to the parent on every marked kind but to the mesh children only from the overhead kind, while a clear carries to the parent and the children; a miss clears a marked mesh. A segment-kind mesh is then asked its segment callback (a 50 cm sphere swept along the segment against its triangles) and an overhead one its ray callback, both from `cut_away`, when the node holds `cut_triangles`; `fade_step` is the fade rule on its own, which the live cut-away drives, and a mesh's fade target is ½ unless its word asks it to fade all the way. It shares [`scene_nodes::cell_spill`] with `light_flood`. The scene's timed track list is `exa_anim::tracks` (a start swaps once and reverse, the step clocks each track) and its grid cells' twelve-slot light lists are `light_flood::CellLights` (a directional light fills every cell, a removal takes the first entry for the light from each cell).
- `light_alias`: the original's per-node light weight (the plain luma test, the gated sphere/oriented-box/shell falloff with its fourth power, the spot's vertical blend and fast power-of-two cone term, the intensity gate and colour luma, and the six-face shadow mask against the node's radius), with the spot constants derived from the record's `(q, p)`; reuses `exa_physics::intersect::point_obb_distance_squared` and `exa_formats::colour::luma`. `reach_with` asks the cell flood's wall gate only after the sphere and box tests (pure tests, so the same answer as the original's gate-first order), and a hit reports whether it got as far as the face mask (the only case the original writes the alias's shadow map word). The live scene's node passes run it through `scene_nodes::Scene::alias_reach`.
- `light_flood`: a directional light fills `width²` cells with an empty window (cells that list it already keep theirs); a re-flooded light is first cleared only from the box of `ceil(radius / 6 m) + 1` cells round its new cell (in the original's 32-bit arithmetic, so an unranged light clears nothing), so a light that moved far keeps stale entries in its old cells, which later floods widen like any entry; the mesh cell and spill test is the scene-node unit's `cell_spill`; `cells_of` gives the cells the light's last flood recorded it in (the cell hook's calls), which the room light lists and the scene's cell hook read.
- `shadow_links`: in tile-grid levels a mesh links only the lights with an alias in its sector (`scene_live::LiveScene::light_aliased`; `RoomLightLists`, rebuilt as each light floods, answers for a light the scene does not hold and feeds the log; the flood runs before the scene's frame so its activation reads the cells; the sector from `scene_live::LiveScene::sector_of`, whose `sector_changes` relink a mesh that moved sector; `--no-room-lights` turns the test off and a log line reports the links it refused). ## Hair passes and the light flood's clear

A cloth whose record carries the hair bit is drawn as two culled passes
(`cloth_draw::draw_hair_passes`): its entity draws the front-culled copy of
its material, a child the back-culled one (`ExaMaterial::second_pass`, kept
out of the blended walk and sorted just after the first). A light's
re-flood clears it from a box around its new place
(`light_flood::CellLights::clear_box`), not from every cell it entered; the
rooms a flood aliases come from the cells that flood recorded
(`cells_of`). The terrain's composite pools take their detail side from
the texture-detail setting.

The terrain tiles' vertex normals are `terrain::manager::window_normal` (the
original's Sobel sums in its order, the factor one eighth over the cell
side, the engine normalise with the fixed approximate reciprocal square
root of DEC-7) over the heights in metres.
